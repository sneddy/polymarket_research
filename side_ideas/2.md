# Side Idea 2

## Compact Latent State Of The Contemporaneous Market System

Core framing:

- we learn a compact latent state that captures task-relevant information from the contemporaneous market system;
- the latent state is not intended to reconstruct all market information, but to provide a sufficient representation for downstream forecasting, trust, and repricing tasks;
- we study whether a low-dimensional bottleneck can preserve the predictive and uncertainty-relevant structure of the market state.

Stronger wording:

- we evaluate whether a compact latent representation can serve as a useful summary of the market's belief state;
- here, "compact" matters more than "complete".

Paper-style formulation:

At each time `t`, we encode the observable market system into a low-dimensional latent state `z_t`.

The goal of `z_t` is not full reconstruction of all contracts, but retention of information sufficient for downstream belief-state tasks.

We test this sufficiency through terminal forecasting, trust estimation, and repricing prediction.

Important protective statement:

We do not assume that a low-dimensional latent state captures the entirety of market knowledge; instead, we ask whether it captures enough of the task-relevant structure to generalize across downstream evaluations.

Why this is promising:

- it gives a unifying object across multiple benchmarks;
- it is more ML-native than a loose collection of tasks;
- it can be evaluated by compactness-performance trade-offs rather than by a single forecasting metric.
